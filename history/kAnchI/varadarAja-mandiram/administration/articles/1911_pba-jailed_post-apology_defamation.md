## Context
Ozhapakkam Ramanujachariar famously converted from TK to VK.

## Summary of Proceedings
### Events
1. The accused, a Thengalai Brahmin aged 20, was charged with defaming the complainant, a Vadagalai Brahmin, by publishing a pamphlet alleging:
- The complainant was the son of a prostitute.
- His mother openly lived with several men.
- He lived on his wife’s prostitution.

2. In Case No. 102/1910, the complainant had earlier charged the accused with defamation in a Sanskrit work. That case was dismissed but later retried by order of the High Court. The accused apologized, and the complainant accepted it on **2 September 1911**.

3. On **6 September 1911**, the accused went to Madras and arranged for the Presidency Press to print a pamphlet (Exhibit A), which was published on **7 September 1911**. Copies were circulated against the complainant.

4. Despite apologizing earlier, the accused deliberately defamed the complainant again. He admitted publication of the pamphlet but claimed it was not defamatory.

5. Witnesses testified that the accused apologized at the complainant’s house and circulated a notice, but he never withdrew the defamatory statements.

6. The pamphlet stated that the complainant had accumulated wealth by “selling his wife without fear or shame.” The court found this clearly defamatory.

---

### Court’s Findings

- The accused’s apology was insincere, as he repeated the defamation.
- Circulated apologies contained no withdrawal of the libel.
- The accused continued to insist the pamphlet was not defamatory.
- Considering his youth and the sectarian tensions between Thengalais and Vadagalais, the court opted for imprisonment rather than a fine (which his followers might pay).

---

### Sentence

The accused, **P.B. Annangaracharya**, is sentenced to **28 days of simple imprisonment under Section 500 IPC**.

---

**Sd. J.R.S. Jackson**  
Joint Magistrate  
**6 January 1912**
